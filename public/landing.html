The identifier was never the name. It is the combination of ordinary attributes that look harmless alone.

The most-cited statistic in data privacy is one two researchers disagree about.

Both agree on the phenomenon. They disagree, by more than twenty-five percentage points, on the sharpest case. It is unresolved.

Linkage does not pick a winner. It settles what a simulator can settle: generate populations where the truth is known, run both methodologies against them, and show where they diverge and why. Every attack is scored against ground truth, so a reader gets 340 of 500 targets identified, never 68% success.

A NIK encodes the quasi-identifier by construction.

Sixteen digits with a published structure: province, kabupaten, kecamatan, date of birth, sequence. Female records add 40 to the day. Region, date of birth and gender are Sweeney's exact triple, and a NIK carries all three.

Masking a NIK the way a card is masked is backwards. Card practice keeps a short tail and hides the rest. Applied here, the habit that keeps the head preserves region, birthdate and gender, which is everything the join needs, while hiding only the sequence number, which identifies almost nothing on its own.

The correct masking destroys the head and may safely keep the tail. The two figures above are computed on the same generated population as the field, and the gap between them is the whole argument.

Every technique in Linkage is textbook and published. The app contributes explanation and measurement, not capability.

Five cases, each one an attack, a defense, and the attack that beats it.

A defense is introduced with the threat model it assumes, then with the attack that defeats it, in that order. The sandbox opens after the fifth.

  • The linkage

    A target and two tables. Neither identifies anyone alone. You join them, and one row resolves to plain ink while everything around it stays ambiguous.

  • k-anonymity, and homogeneity

    Generalise until every record is indistinguishable from at least k minus one others. Then find the class where all of them share one diagnosis, and learn it without identifying anyone.

  • l-diversity, and skewness

    Enforce diversity, then find the class where it is technically satisfied and the distribution still gives near-certainty.

  • The aggregates

    No record-level data at all. Two published averages, one subtraction, one person singled out.

  • The budget

    Differential privacy holds regardless of auxiliary knowledge. Its price is noise, and a budget that composes and cannot be refilled.

    Every control exposed, with the privacy and utility curves as a shape you move along rather than a sentence you read. The assessor is the one mode meant for a real dataset, and it takes column descriptions, never a row.

What it will not do.

These come first because they define the product rather than qualify it.

  • It accepts no data

    No file input, no drag-and-drop, no paste, and no code path that takes a record from outside. An app that ingests a dataset and helps you re-identify the people in it is a re-identification tool, whatever the surrounding copy says. A test greps the built bundle and fails the deploy on a hit.

  • It keeps ground truth

    Because the populations are generated, the app knows who every record actually is. That is what lets an attack report 340 of 500 targets identified and let you inspect the 160 that failed. Uploaded data could tell you nothing, which would make it a worse teacher, not a better one.

  • Nothing leaves the device

    No network at runtime, no analytics, no accounts. Given the subject this is not a nicety. An app about data disclosure that phones home would be self-refuting.

The field is the argument. It takes about a minute.
